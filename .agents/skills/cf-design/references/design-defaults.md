# Design-default awareness

Use this reference to notice unexamined defaults in a new or materially
reshaped user-facing surface. It is not a checklist, theme catalog, scoring
system, or set of prohibited styles. A familiar pattern may be exactly right
for a brief; the failure is using it without establishing why it serves this
product and audience.

## How to use this reference

1. Identify a pattern that appears in the proposed direction.
2. Ask what product, audience, content, or platform evidence supports it.
3. Keep it when it is the clearest fit and record the rationale.
4. Refine or replace it when it is merely the easiest available completion.
5. Never reject an operator-requested direction solely because it resembles a
   common pattern. Give honest counsel, then respect the settled direction.

## Common default clusters

### Marketing-page formula

- oversized generic claim followed by a small label or eyebrow;
- gradient accent, floating mockup, three feature cards, statistics, then CTA;
- numbered sections whose order carries no information;
- proof bars, logos, testimonials, or metrics that were never supplied.

Inspect whether the page's structure reflects the subject and the user's
decision journey. Never invent proof to satisfy a layout.

### Warm editorial product

- cream background, dark serif display type, terracotta or olive accent;
- generous whitespace and soft photography regardless of the subject;
- artisanal language applied to products whose value is precision or speed.

This can be appropriate. Confirm that the intended register, audience, and
content earn it.

### Dark technical product

- near-black surfaces, acid green or electric purple accents;
- terminal fragments, grids, glow, glass panels, and monospaced labels;
- simulated browser, phone, IDE, or terminal chrome used as decoration.

Ask whether the product is genuinely technical, whether the chrome communicates
real behavior, and whether contrast, legibility, and hierarchy survive the
effect.

### Bento-dashboard reflex

- every fact isolated into an equal rounded card;
- visually varied card sizes with no information-priority rationale;
- charts, badges, and status pills added because the layout expects them;
- the same desktop card grid compressed rather than redesigned responsively.

Use grouping and cards only when they express meaningful boundaries or actions.
Structure is information, not a decoration layer.

### Decorative motion

- identical reveal animation on every element;
- parallax, cursor effects, or ambient movement without a product purpose;
- motion that hides loading or state changes instead of explaining them.

Prefer one purposeful moment over scattered effects. Respect reduced-motion
preferences and preserve comprehension without animation.

### Neutral-system inertia

- safe defaults retained everywhere even when the brief calls for a distinct
  product character;
- one type family, one spacing rhythm, and one component treatment regardless
  of content role;
- platform conventions ignored in pursuit of cross-platform sameness.

Existing systems deserve a strong presumption, not immunity from evidence.
Extend them coherently when the product has a real unmet need.

## Critique lenses

Use the lenses that fit the surface:

- **Brief fit:** does the direction express the creator's actual intent?
- **Audience fit:** does it support the user's job, context, trust, and
  expectations?
- **Hierarchy:** does structure show what matters and what happens next?
- **Interaction:** are state, feedback, recovery, and destructive actions clear?
- **Specificity:** do choices come from this subject rather than a generic
  category aesthetic?
- **Coherence:** do type, colour, layout, imagery, density, and motion reinforce
  one direction?
- **Restraint:** does every visual and interactive device earn its place?
- **System fit:** does it extend existing foundations rather than fragment them?
- **Accessibility:** does the design work for relevant input, perception,
  cognition, viewport, and motion needs?
- **Fidelity:** does the implementation preserve the settled direction and
  behavior?

State the evidence behind a criticism. “This is generic” or “I dislike this”
alone is not a review finding.
