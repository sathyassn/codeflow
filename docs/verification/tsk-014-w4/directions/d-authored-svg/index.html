TSK-014 W4 · contract altitude · direction D

Authored SVG under a runtime service contract

Authorship stays with the agent. The document carries inline SVG it drew, and the runtime supplies what the sandboxed escape block cannot: token substitution, mode resolution, a declared responsive variant set, viewBox sizing, and an enforced legend and description.

Expressive reach is maximal — anything an agent can draw. Both figures below are the agent's own SVG, qualified and token-substituted by service.js at load.

The plan, drawn by hand

Two variants were authored: a wide one and a narrow one. The service picks between them and follows the document's mode. Nothing about the composition was decided by the product.

What it cannot express — and why that is the wrong question

There is no subject this direction refuses. That is not a strength on its own: the contract has no model of what a figure means, so it cannot tell a good encoding from a bad one, cannot check that every drawn mark is keyed, cannot detect a collision, and cannot reuse anything between two documents. The three refusals it can make are structural, and they are attempted below rather than described.

Nothing accumulates

The same subject, authored a second time under the same contract by the same rules. Both are valid. Neither constrains the other, and a reader moving between two documents has to learn the encoding twice.

Two authors, one contract, two encodings, no shared authority. Multiply by every explanation the product ever produces.

The security delta, stated rather than assumed

Today an html block is served into a bare-sandbox iframe at an opaque origin under default-src 'none'. This direction moves author-supplied SVG into the parent document, because that is the only way it can reach the document's tokens and resolved mode. That is a real change in blast radius — same-origin DOM, the review page's own stylesheet, and the feedback surface all become reachable by a markup bug rather than by a sandbox escape. The qualifier above is a parser-level allowlist, which is where the existing safe_html.rs boundary already sits; whether it is sufficient at the new origin is a security question this board raises and does not answer.
