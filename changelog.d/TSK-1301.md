### Fixed

<!-- codeflow:release-impact minor -->
- **A slow destination no longer refuses an ordinary push.** The pre-push
  hook, `codeflow ci`, `task status` and the id sync asked the destination
  for its default branch with a fixed 10 s per attempt, and GitHub answered
  in 11.3 s and 11.6 s, so pushes refused with `ci.destination_unavailable`.
  Three new policy keys set the waits: `git.remote_query_timeout_seconds`
  (default 30), `git.remote_query_attempts` (default 3) and
  `git.remote_fetch_timeout_seconds` (default 120, the earlier fixed fetch
  wait). Each takes a whole number of at least 1 with no other ceiling, so a
  very large value means effectively no limit; any other value refuses at
  load, naming the key. The pre-push hook, `task status` and the id sync read
  them from the default branch's committed policy, never from a pushed head;
  `codeflow ci` reads them from the authority its caller or range selects,
  as it does every rule. A destination that does not answer in time still
  refuses, and the message names the wait, the attempts, the time measured
  and the keys. The session guards keep their own 10 s wait for each such
  lookup, and their 20 s deadline bounds the whole command: when the remote's
  tracking refs are missing and the remote does not answer in that time, the
  guard refuses and names `git fetch <remote>`, after which it reads local
  refs and needs no network.
<!-- codeflow:release-impact patch -->
- **The guards' recovery fetch allowance covers only a plain fetch.** While
  the policy authority cannot be resolved, both session guards let a fetch of
  a configured remote through before their integrity checks. They judged a
  normalised form of the line, so a line doing more than the fetch could
  qualify and skip those checks. They now judge the line as written and give
  the allowance only to one plain fetch; anything else gets the normal
  refusal.
