# EPC-014: public-release documentation and presentation

## Summary

CodeFlow's documentation is the first thing a public reader meets, and today it
misstates its own contracts: the portal re-renders Markdown instead of composing
it, the orient layer carries stale history and wrong pins, and the scaffolded
templates, presets and help strings disagree with the code they describe. This
epic makes the sources correct and the right length, makes the portal a visual
presentation of those sources under one written doctrine, and repairs the
deeper contract defects the docs pass exposed. Done is: every changed page
reviewed rendered in three skins across light and dark, the full Rust gate and
both validators green, and an EPC-014 PR prepared for a human to land.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1 One shared utility-presentation doctrine governs both cf-docs-portal and
      cf-present, states that the portal composes sources visually and that
      re-rendered Markdown fails, and lists only carriers the adapter actually
      supports; `docs/architecture/utility-presentation.md` is its durable home
      and ADR-0063 records precedence over ADR-0053 append-only.
- AC-2 The three skins each carry their own identity in light and dark, are
      identical across the live portal, the starter and the embedded present
      distribution, and hold text and muted at or above 4.5:1 on canvas and
      surface in all six pairs; evidence: the six-pair token contract test
      including a starter to live equality check, a computed contrast table,
      and Fable's rendered review of three skins in both modes at 1280 and 390.
- AC-3 Every explanatory page of the guide (orient, system and reference)
      carries the altitude trio with a figure in Concept, a labeled structure
      in Architecture and lookup tables in Technical, each framed by prose,
      in a reading order from what it is to how it is operated; decisions,
      epics, tasks and specs are pointed to as folders and are not portal
      pages; evidence: the rendered review per page per altitude and the
      page-class gate. Orient sources state one governing claim per page,
      carry no v1/v2 history, and name the correct Node pins (portal 24.18.0,
      present 26.4.0), Codex effort high, and 22 subcommands including
      `estimate`; `validate --docs` and rendered review confirm each.
- AC-4 README, CONTRIBUTING, SECURITY, CHANGELOG, releasing and release-checklist
      are internally consistent; the pending 3.0.0 consolidation is reconciled
      only by the existing release tooling, never by hand, or deferred with the
      reason recorded.
- AC-5 Nine enumerated contract/scaffold defects each have an expected behaviour
      and a named positive/negative regression test, and the full `codeflow
      test`, `validate --docs` and `doctor --check managed-drift` pass.
- AC-6 `browser-verify` fails a build in which any eligible source lacks what
      its page class requires: every explanatory page the altitude trio with a figure, the record
      pointer page its folder table, and no per-record route when records are
      off; proven by fixtures where one compliant page cannot mask a
      noncompliant one and where a page shipped as Markdown under the shell
      fails and is named.
- AC-7 The installed binary from the integrated candidate is qualified on
      generated greenfield and brownfield sample repositories at the minimal,
      standard and full tiers, with every subcommand recorded passed, failed or
      unavailable against its documented contract, a delegate canary and one
      pipeline run included, and the candidate commit and binary SHA-256
      recorded; evidence: the qualification matrix appended to
      `docs/verification/releases/v3.0.0.md`, where a material failure blocks
      qualification.
- AC-8 The combined diff passes editorial review, Codex review and Grok
      adversarial review; the PR to main is prepared but landed by a human only
      on actually-green required checks.
