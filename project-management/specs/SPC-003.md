## Summary

Pin down how language, voice, appearance modes, and themes differ across the
product-design, temporary-presentation, and durable-documentation planes. This
prevents generic model language and utility defaults from flattening product
voice while keeping the shared editorial doctrine concise and consistent.

## Delta against current capability

**MODIFIED — CAP-010/CAP-013 design and artifact judgment.** Language and
appearance become explicit contextual design dimensions and evaluation
subjects. `cf-editorial-review` remains the single shared diagnosis/review
mechanism; surface skills add only their generation-time defaults and distinct
obligations.

## Behavior

1. `cf-design` treats verbal language as part of the consuming product's
   experience. It inspects project voice, approved examples, audience literacy,
   use context, trust/risk, platform, accessibility, internationalization, and
   localization before settling material copy direction.
2. A material `DESIGN_INTENT` may include collapsible `LANGUAGE_AND_VOICE` and
   `APPEARANCE_MODES` fields. They cover only decisions that steer the surface;
   established-system conformance or a genuine N/A remains compact.
3. `cf-design` considers light, dark, high-contrast, system-following, manual
   override, persistence, imagery/chart behavior, and reduced motion where the
   product and medium make them applicable. It does not ship or recommend fixed
   CodeFlow product themes; themes belong to the consuming product.
4. Product language and visual expression support the same experience target.
   Labels, navigation, onboarding, validation, empty/error/destructive/recovery
   states, consent, and help are verified where applicable.
5. `cf-present` uses session-aware, plain, direct explanatory language by
   default. `cf-docs-portal` uses the consuming project's documentation voice
   when established and otherwise uses plain, precise, topic-led exposition.
   Procedures prefer direct imperatives. Second person is contextual rather
   than mechanically prohibited; fabricated dialogue or familiarity is not.
6. Utility titles and navigation labels describe the actual subject, decision,
   or action. Product titles remain governed by product evidence and may differ.
7. Emoji is absent by default in utility surfaces and valid in product language
   only when evidenced, functional, or explicitly directed. No surface uses
   decorative model-associated emoji as synthetic personality.
8. Truth, evidence, exact meaning, and policy outrank every voice or tone
   preference. Conflicts are surfaced rather than rewritten into false
   confidence.

## Interfaces and formats

- Extend the existing `DESIGN_INTENT` contract rather than creating a parallel
  language checklist or mandatory document.
- Keep shared title, emoji, fabricated-personality, and editorial-smell doctrine
  under `cf-editorial-review`; other skills link to it and retain only their
  distinct defaults.
- Add semantic evaluation requirements to the existing model-evaluation kit;
  deterministic checks cover structural/schema integrity only.

## Edge cases

- A requested tone that hides risk or overstates evidence is rejected with the
  conflict explained.
- A bounded conformance change does not expand into a product voice exercise or
  boilerplate `DESIGN_INTENT`.
- A product with an established playful or emoji-bearing voice is not flattened
  into CodeFlow utility prose.
- A localization obligation is not claimed verified from English-only copy.
- A technical tutorial may use second person when it improves reader clarity;
  a conceptual portal page does not invent a conversational narrator.
- Modes are considered in context; unsupported media or an explicit single-mode
  product records the reason rather than receiving a meaningless toggle.
