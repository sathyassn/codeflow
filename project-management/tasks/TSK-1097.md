# TSK-1097: The immediately obvious bar is stated in both skills and drawn by the figure guidance

## Description

The operator's principle of 2026-10-30 (20:15, relayed by the coordinator)
for `cf-present` and `cf-docs-portal` alike: information that is
immediately obvious, visual plus supporting text, with the right context,
a leading anchor and an order that spares the reader from connecting the
dots; visuals and colour used in useful ways that convey things faster
without losing detail or the real complexity. The operator also asked for
Anthropic's artifact guidance (artifact-design, artifact-diagramming and
dataviz, read on 2026-10-30) to be compared with the two skills and its
good parts adopted. TSK-445 (PR 344) already carries the method's anchor,
section leads, figure-first order and the artifact-design and diagramming
lessons; TSK-446 fixes the portal's page order. This task closes what
neither covers and writes the principle as a checkable bar in both skills.
The operator's feedback of 2026-10-30 (20:40) sharpened the bar on three
points, all of which apply to `cf-present`, `cf-docs-portal` and the
status report alike: a detail section is layered information and opens
to content presented as well as the first path, never text swept into a
box; a table that is needed is elegant and restrained; and a document or
page answers the questions its reader brings, in the order they arise,
so the reader never connects the dots.

Outcome: **both skills state the immediately obvious bar in checkable
terms in their composition gates and review rubrics, with the encoded

The bar, as both skills will state it:

```text
  anchor: what this is, why it matters, where it stands      (first screen)
     |
     v
  one orienting visual naming the parts later ones zoom into
     |
     v
  each section: question in a lead line -> the figure or encoded visual
                that answers it -> short acting text
     |
     v
  tables encode state, extent or emphasis, restrained, in or out of a
  disclosure; a disclosure opens to the same craft as the first path
     |
     v
  a cold reader names the overall state in a few seconds; detail reachable
  and, when opened, as clear as the first screen
```

Figure: the order of a document or page under the bar, from the anchor to
the reachable detail.

Decisions, each reversible and disclosed here:

- The bar's checkable items: (a) the first screen carries the anchor and
  an orienting visual; (b) every section opens with a lead line and
  carries a figure or an encoded visual that answers it, or says in its
  lead why none applies; (c) a table in the first reading path encodes
  state, extent or emphasis; (d) every state differs on two non-hue
  channels and has an accessible name; (e) a cold reader names the
  overall state and the main blocker or relationship in a few seconds,
  recorded as an answer; (f) every summarised value keeps its exact value
  and source reachable; (g) a disclosure or details panel opens to a lead
  line and a figure or an encoded table, never to a bare paragraph with a
  plain table or to a wall of text, so the layered detail is as clear as
  the first screen, and its closed summary says what is inside with a
  mark or a count where one applies; (h) a table is restrained: one encoding per column,
  at most two tones beside the accent, the kit's quiet surface, a legend
  of only what is used, and no decoration beyond the vocabulary
  (TSK-1096 AC-8 holds the renderer to it); (i) the author names the
  reader and records the questions that reader brings, in the order they
  arise, and the sections follow that recorded list with each lead naming
  its question; the bar fixes no universal sequence, since a status report
  (TSK-1098's list) and a capability page bring different readers; it
  requires that the list exists, is recorded in the document's or page's
  summary, and is followed; (j) (added 2026-10-31, the operator's
  feedback on consistency) a recurring piece uses the catalog's component
  set, adds a new section kind once with a specimen rather than
  improvising it in an `html` stage, and differs from its predecessor
  only where the facts differ, so consecutive versions look alike; for a
  present document of a recurring kind (a status report, a design
  review) this means the shell's layout contract and view-kind registry
  (TSK-1120), a `topic` so a repeat revises the same document with change
  marks (TSK-1121) and stable block ids; a portal page keeps its
  committed source and route identity under the portal content contract
  (`cf-docs-portal/references/content-contract.md`) and takes only the
  composition and consistency guidance, never a topic or a session
  (Codex round 8, F3). Items (a) to (d), (g) and (h) are mechanical and
  the checks report them; the mechanical half of (j) is reported by the
  checks TSK-1120 (an `html` stage where the registry kind names a
  catalog carrier, a `custom` view) and TSK-1121 (a missing `topic` on a
  registry-kind document) own, this task stating only the guidance; (e),
  (f), (i) and the rest of (j) are the reviewer's judged evidence, with
  (i) checked against the recorded list. Two further rules of craft, from the four
  reviews of the status report of 2026-10-30: every state or location
  word is defined where first used (a subtitle, a lead or a caption),
  and a caption carries one new fact (the last event, the basis) and
  never restates the figure or describes the table.
- The method text's sentence that puts tables in disclosures (TSK-445
  AC-3) is superseded: a plain table is reference detail and sits only in
  a figure twin, never in the first reading path and never alone in a
  disclosure; an encoded table may lead a section when a comparison or
  lookup is its better shape. This task lands after TSK-445; TSK-445's
  own criteria are unchanged.
- The portal's rule that a Technical table never stands in for its figure
  (`cf-docs-portal/references/visual-craft.md`) stays for plain tables; an
  encoded table bound as a declaration may be a panel's carrier when the
  altitude contract's families do not fit, with the reason in its
  caption.
