# TSK-158: present note markers on adjacent lines stay apart

## Description

In the present review surface, the note markers of two notes whose anchors
are closer than a marker's height sit on top of each other. Fable found it in
the TSK-071 round 4 design review (P3-10): on the annotation fixture, the
markers of element notes on two adjacent diff lines overlap in the gutter,
badge 2 covering most of badge 1 (36 px lines, 36 px markers). The render
(`t071-fable/r4/renders/slate-dark-rail.png`) sits in a session scratchpad;
it is copied into this task's evidence when the task starts. `markerPlacement` in `web/src/chrome.tsx` places each marker
from its own anchor only, so the same happens for any notes whose anchors
are close: adjacent code or prose lines, or two notes on one line. Make each
marker readable and clickable on its own while it still points at its
anchor. Non-goal: changing what a marker looks like or how notes anchor.

## Affected surfaces and interfaces

`crates/codeflow-present/web/src/chrome.tsx` marker placement and, if
needed, `styles.css`; `web/scripts/browser-check.mjs` or
`matrix-browser-check.mjs`; the present rebuild and its checksum. No server,
envelope or CLI change.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1 When two or more notes anchor within one marker height of each other,
      their markers do not overlap, and each stays where the placement rule
      for that width puts it, beside or above its own anchor. When that row
      is full (to the page edge, or on a desktop to the sections list, which
      no marker covers), the extra markers continue down the gutter on a
      desktop and below the line on a phone, each in the first free place
      below its line, at an offset stated in the check; evidence: a browser
      check that saves notes on two adjacent diff lines, two adjacent code
      lines and enough notes on one prose line to fill its row, and asserts
      that the marker boxes are disjoint, that each marker's vertical range
      meets its anchor's line or an offset stated in the check, and that a
      marker leaves its row only when no gap in the row holds it.
- AC-2 Each marker still opens its own note from a click at its centre, at
      desktop width and at 375 px; evidence: the same check clicks every
      marker and asserts the note it opens.
- AC-3 The annotation matrix, `npm run check`, the browser checks,
      `cargo test -p codeflow-present` and `cargo test -p codeflow-cli --test
      present_cli` stay green and the present checksum matches a clean
      rebuild; evidence: the recorded output with the head SHA.
- AC-4 The design primary reviews the marker layout on the fixture in the
      three skins and two modes; evidence: the review record.

## Closeout

Note markers whose anchors lie closer than a marker's height no longer
overlap. Each stays level with its own line, or just above it on a phone,
and leaves that line's row only when the row is full, as AC-1 (amended in
PR 686) states. Evidence is at `b5b83ad65` on `task/TSK-158-marker-overlap`.

- Placement (AC-1): `markerPlacements` in `web/src/chrome.tsx` places the
  markers top to bottom against every marker already placed. A marker whose
  place is taken first moves down while it still covers 10 px of its line,
  then tries the place just past each placed marker, nearest first, away
  from its anchor. Only when no place in its row is free does it continue
  from its own place: down the gutter on a desktop, from just below its
  line on a phone. No marker starts within 2 px of the fixed sections list;
  where the list narrows the gutter, a marker closes its 8 px gap, up to
  touching its line's box but never covering it (Fable's optional 2 px gap
  does not fit there: 48 px less the 2 px clearance is the 46 px marker).
- Check (AC-1, AC-2): the annotation matrix's close markers step saves 13
  notes: two adjacent diff lines, two adjacent code lines, and nine on one
  prose line, three on the same words, so markers 10 to 13 are the wider
  two-digit ones. At 1280 px, at 375 px, and after widening from 375 to
  1280 with the rail closed, it re-measures the anchors and asserts that no
  two markers meet, none sits on its anchor or within 2 px of the sections
  list, and each takes the width's placement mode. An overflowed marker
  must end at its own place, derived from the block and the page, and no
  free stretch of its row may hold it; load-time negative controls cover a
  sparse row, a middle gap, a packed row and a chain moved to the row's far
  end. A click at each marker's centre opens its own note at both widths.
- Earlier placements fail the step: markers 1 and 2 overlap at
  `334a9f580`, markers 5 and 10 at `7a87cff0a`, marker 10 enters the
  sections list at `52c2ff781`; an overflow moved to the left bound fails
  at marker 6.
- Rows seen: at 1280 px the gutter beside the sections list holds one
  marker per line, and the rest continue down it; at 375 px eight markers
  fit the row above the prose line and the thirteenth goes below it.
- Gates (AC-3) at `b5b83ad65`: `npm run check` reproducible;
  `check:matrix`, `check:browser` (and with Firefox and WebKit chip
  engines), `check:entities` pass; `cargo test -p codeflow-present` 155
  passed; `present_cli` 12 passed and 1 ignored; clippy and fmt clean.
- Design (AC-4): Fable reviewed the layout in the three skins and two
  modes at `334a9f580` and approved round 2 at `7a87cff0a`, choosing the
  downward overflow and the strict sections-list bound, and approved the
  narrowed gutter rule at `80fa1e575`.
- Decision, not a task: while the sections list shows, the desktop gutter
  holds one marker per line, so a second note on a line sits one marker
  below it. Fable, the design primary, decided to accept this; the fix, if
  it is ever needed, is a layout change that reserves a second gutter lane
  beside the list. Revisit trigger: the first operator or reviewer report
  that a marker below its line was read as another line's, or two or more
  notes on one line in at least one in five delivered reviews, counted from
  the present review history when the EPC-016 line is reviewed for release.
