# TSK-075: autonomy reference and its decision record

## Description

Write the one reference an agent cites to know whether a step is its own to
take, and reduce the seven operator-owned lists to that one list plus
pointers.

`cf-method/references/autonomy.md` carries three parts, taken from section 4
of Fable's audit and tightened in review:

- the short statement: a task runs from its settled outcome to its finish
  line (build, verify, review, open the pull request, follow it, report
  readiness); the agent settles discoverable facts and reversible choices
  from evidence and says what it chose; it stops only at a listed gate; it
  never stops to offer, to confirm a safe step it already has authority
  for, to report progress, or because a peer, tool or job is red; a stop
  halts only that action, keeps the rest moving, and brings evidence,
  options and a recommendation; a missing seat, tool or credit is reduced
  assurance to record, not a question;
- the ladder figure, four rows: act; notify and act (one line in the
  running report, not a pause); ask (one question, options, a
  recommendation, other work continues); hard gate (wait for a human);
- the decision table, one row per situation with its source, covering at
  least the audit's 25 rows, and naming seats by role (the Claude judgment
  primary, the Claude design and review route) so it does not depend on
  concrete selectors.

The reference owns the only full operator-owned list: intent or public
behavior the brief does not fix, taste, authority or scope beyond the
brief, spend, credentials, anything sent outside the conversation, a
protected-branch merge, production, a delete nothing can restore, and a
step with no way back. It also owns the trust prompt rule in the
workspace's words: the agent answers a trust prompt for its task's own
project folder or a disposable sample its harness created, and any other
folder goes to the operator. The hard-gate list in the contract's "Match
the gate" bullet stays where it is and the reference cites it.

Reduce the lists. The standard contract's blocker bullet swaps
"Escalate only an external dependency or operator-owned choice, with a
recommendation." (85 bytes) for "Escalate only a gate in
`cf-method/references/autonomy.md`, with a recommendation." (82 bytes),
keeping every pinned clause; the planning bullet keeps its compact list and
gains no bytes. The minimal contract has no skill tree, so the short
statement itself must fit inline; trade an equal byte count inside the file
(the minimal escalation sentence and the densest clause of the
legitimate-intent bullet) without deleting a pinned clause, or record the
operator's ratchet decision in EPC-018 if they choose that instead.
`CLAUDE.md.tmpl` gains one pointer sentence. `workflow-lifecycle.md` gains
one pointer in "Establish the route" (a request for a change selects
implementation to the readiness report) and one in the closeout section,
and its hard-gate paragraph moves to the reference behind a pointer.
`quality-contract.md` points its operator-decision sentence at the
reference. `cf-plan/SKILL.md` keeps its clarity gate and adds "per
`autonomy.md`" so the two cannot drift. The orchestrator preflight list is
TSK-076's; the workspace list is downstream.

ADR-0070, "autonomous operation with judgment", records the reference as
the owner, the statement, the ladder and the list; it supersedes nothing,
cites ADR-0034, ADR-0038 and ADR-0066, and names the orchestrator
reconciliation stop that TSK-076 narrows. Add `autonomy.md` to the
scaffold manifest for the standard and full tiers so `codeflow update`
carries it, and add a test that asserts, per file, the pointer or the
verbatim clause in the standard contract, `CLAUDE.md.tmpl`,
`workflow-lifecycle.md`, `quality-contract.md` and `cf-plan/SKILL.md`.

Non-goal: a new gate, a change to the hard-gate list, or any skill
behaviour change beyond pointers (TSK-076 owns those).
