# TSK-1063: Every Windows test partition finishes well inside its job limit

## Description

Source: PR 392's run 40775926015. On its first attempt, "windows tests
(5 of 12)" (job 123127783463) hit the job's 30-minute limit at 480 of
its 482 tests (corrected from 352 after Codex review 01 of PR 393), while
the other eleven partitions finished in 8 to 17 minutes (read from the
run's job times on 2026-10-28). A timed-out partition fails the
`windows` verdict, and under this repository's "hosted green before the
next landing" rule that holds every ordinary landing on the line.

This repository splits its Windows nextest suite into 12 partitions by
measured duration (TSK-265): `scripts/windows-partitions.py` plans them
from `scripts/windows-test-times.json`, and each `windows-tests` matrix
job in `.github/workflows/codeflow-ci.yml` runs one partition with
`timeout-minutes: 30`. The plan at this line's tip still estimates about
5.6 minutes for every partition (`windows-partitions.py plan`), while
real partitions take 8 to 30 minutes and more. Leads for the
investigation, none confirmed:

- the timing data was measured on runs 38102896319 to 38111523049 and
  last changed on 2026-10-14, and tests added since have no measurement;
- a test binary the data does not know runs in one "rest" partition, and
  an unmeasured test of a split binary runs in that binary's home
  partition (`windows-partitions.py:137-146`), so new slow tests pile up
  in whichever partition receives them;
- nothing checks the plan against a run, so the drift showed only as a
  timeout.

Further evidence, added 2026-10-29 from the coordinator's reading of
later runs:

- On the line, shard 5 takes 24.7 of its 30 minutes (the coordinator's
  reading). Job times read on 2026-10-29: 25.2 minutes in line run
  40807522540 (job 123232552459) and 22.4 minutes in line run
  40825861843 (job 123290157555).
- On PR 386 (run 40798842036), the first attempt of shard 5 (job
  123204313340) timed out at 328 of 482 tests with no test failure.
  "windows journeys" and the `windows` verdict then failed because the
  shard's report was missing. The second attempt (job 123217810982)
  passed in 23.3 minutes. "37 percent slower" is the coordinator's
  estimate of that first attempt's runner against the rerun; the
  calculation is not recorded, so AC-1 recomputes it from per-test
  durations of the two attempts.
- On PR 385, a cancellation while shard 5 ran caused the same
  "windows journeys" failure.

So a normal runner leaves shard 5 under 20 percent headroom, a slower
one times it out, and a missing report is read downstream as a failure
of its tests.

The fix balances by measured duration, not test count, keeps every
partition well under the job limit with a stated margin, and makes drift
visible before it times out a job. Raising the job timeout alone is not
a fix.

Relation to TSK-384: TSK-384 (issue #203, and issue #352 item 3) lets
an adopter split a gate target into its own jobs and run the managed
gate as a matrix of shards generated from the test config, in the
shipped workflow `assets/base/ci/codeflow-ci.yml`. This task changes
only this repository's own Windows test partitions
(`.github/workflows/codeflow-ci.yml`, `scripts/windows-partitions.py`
and its data). It does not touch the managed workflow, and TSK-384 does
not touch these partitions. The Linux coverage shards share the same
planner through `scripts/coverage-shards.py` (TSK-270), so the
investigation checks them as a sibling.

## Outcome

After this merges, a maintainer can rely on every Windows test partition of this repository's CI finishing with a stated margin under its job limit, and on drift being reported before a partition times out.

### Planned against real

The plan at the line tip (`windows-partitions.py plan`) gives every
partition 5.6 minutes (22.4 test minutes, 269 in all). The six line runs
measure 441 test minutes in all (median per test across the runs), 64 percent
more. In the newest run (40882623406), per partition, tests / test minutes
summed over tests (JUnit test minutes in brackets): 1: 291 / 51.0 (12.9),
2: 332 / 42.8 (10.8), 3: 126 / 29.4 (8.3), 4: 181 / 30.3 (8.8),
5: 496 / 103.5 (26.8), 6: 105 / 33.6 (9.1), 7: 2977 / 45.2 (11.9),
8: 41 / 22.3 (6.2), 9: 44 / 23.3 (7.0), 10: 614 / 27.6 (8.8),
11: 79 / 28.3 (7.8), 12: 226 / 33.0 (9.6). A partition's test minutes are
its summed test seconds over 4 threads (51.0 minutes gives 12.9; 103.5 gives
26.8), so the planner's model holds; its data is what drifted.

Why, by cause:

- The stale data is the main cause. Binaries the data knows have grown:
  `ci_cli` 11.0 to 36.1 summed minutes, `hooks_cli` 5.6 to 29.9,
  `acceptance_cli` 8.6 to 32.0, `codeflow-core` 15.6 to 38.7,
  `acceptance_journey` 6.2 to 26.5, `installed_cli_journey` 3.5 to 11.8,
  `release_line_cli` 16.0 to 20.4, `scaffold_test` 2.3 to 6.1. A whole
  binary stays in one partition, so the growth lands where the binary sits.
  The data stores no test counts, so how much of each growth is new tests
  and how much slower tests cannot be separated.
- Partition 5 also took "the rest". `Plan.rest` is the partition with the
  smallest estimate, and with every estimate equal to 5.6 it is partition 5
  (`windows-partitions.py:137-146` rule). Twenty-four test binaries the data
  does not know ran there: 14.1 summed test minutes (3.2 percent of the
  suite), among them `update_canary_sweep` 3.7, `follow_cli` 2.4,
  `record_outcome_cli` 1.4, `integrate_merge_cli` 1.0, `coord_store` 0.9.
  Unnamed new slow tests of split binaries also stay in the binary's home
  partition: `acceptance_journey`'s five slowest unnamed tests (164 to 246
  seconds each) are in partition 5.
- Partition 5 therefore holds `ci_cli` (36.1), `acceptance_journey` (26.5),
  `installed_cli_journey` (11.8) and the rest, 92.4 summed minutes against
  22.4 planned.
- Nothing compares a run with the plan, so the drift showed only as a
  timeout.

### Runner speed and spread

- Setup per job (job minutes minus JUnit minutes): median 1.1, largest 1.9
  over 72 partition jobs of the six line runs.
- Partition 5 job time across the six line runs: standard deviation 2.1
  minutes (8 percent), range 22.4 to 27.9.
- PR 386 attempt 1 against attempt 2 (recomputed from the PASS lines of the
  two jobs): the 328 tests attempt 1 finished took 112.2 summed minutes
  there and 77.1 in attempt 2, a ratio of 1.46 (the coordinator's 37 percent
  was low); attempt 1 finished them in 28.6 wall minutes, attempt 2 in 19.5
  (1.47). The median per-test ratio over tests of 5 seconds or more is 1.13,
  so the slow run was slower mostly on the long tests.
- PR 397 manual run job 123491332785 against the line run 40882623406: its
  370 finished tests took 113.3 summed minutes against 93.9 for the same
  tests, a ratio of 1.21.
- Partition 7 in run 40889143031 took 22.4 minutes against 12 to 14 in
  every other run, a ratio of about 1.7 on a normal-sized partition.

### Margin held

A partition is planned at 12 test minutes or less (the estimate: summed
seconds over 4 threads, or the serial group's seconds when larger). Setup
takes at most 2 job minutes. The job bound is 24 minutes (AC-6, 80 percent
of the 30 minute limit). Planned 12 plus setup 2 is 14 job minutes on a
typical runner; the worst runner factors read above hold under the bound:
1.21 gives 16.5, 1.46 gives 19.5, 1.7 gives 22.4. A run check fails when a
partition's test minutes plus 2 pass 24, so drift is reported before the
30 minute limit. The suite's 441 test minutes over 12 partitions plan about
9.2 test minutes each, so 12 partitions need no change to the matrix.

### Linux coverage shards (sibling check)

The two shards share the planner and are planned at 5.0 minutes each
(20.1 test minutes); the six line runs measure 1608 and 4667 tests at 8.4
to 13.7 and 7.3 to 11.9 JUnit minutes, 10.6 to 18.6 job minutes (setup

against 40 planned) and an uneven split in single runs (53.0 against 28.6
summed minutes in run 40839551888). The coverage data is refreshed with the
Windows data so it carries test counts for the same check.
