# TSK-341: Reviewed predecessor chains have consistent claim and push decisions

## Description

Issue: #216. `workgraph/work_start.rs:1592` resolves stacked pins by iterating only `task.depends_on` (`:1622`), and returns those pins at `:1666`. It does not recursively preserve the verified predecessor's own stacked authority. This is related to proposal F by transported provenance, but concerns unlanded reviewed work rather than landed completions.

Outcome: **A claim accepted on a reviewed predecessor chain remains pushable under the same verified pins, or claim refuses the unsupported chain before creating unusable work.**

Source inspection is from the accepted triage at `c5c6f6f671d2bf67a77113c656b3be446fb0bea1`; runtime reproductions remain implementation evidence.

Widened 2026-10-26 for issue #332 (CodeFlow 3.1.1), the converse
disagreement. A completed, unlanded predecessor that merges its target
cleanly to stay current can no longer be claimed on: `review_covers_pin`
(`crates/codeflow-core/src/workgraph/work_start.rs`) refuses any merge
in the span between the reviewed commit and the pin, and
`PinBranches::pin_name` refuses the reviewed pre-merge commit once the
branch has advanced. The predecessor's own push accepts the same
history through its acceptance binding (R-60, a clean re-merge of its
target), so claim and push judge one history two ways. AC-5 makes claim
reuse the push's rule.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1 Reproduce A -> B -> C with A's criteria change inside B's reviewed head. Claim, start, pre-push and CI agree; a zero-delta C branch is not newly blamed for A's reviewed range.
- AC-2 If chains are supported, validate every link's exact review, identity, target and ancestry; detect cycles, conflicting tips, unavailable evidence and moved pins. Never trust an ancestor merely because B contains it.
- AC-3 If the chosen bounded behavior is early refusal, name the unsupported predecessor and sanctioned recovery at claim time; document that limit consistently. Do not accept a claim and discover the same known incompatibility only during its push.
- AC-4 (journey) Installed commands exercise a two-deep chain and a broken-link control against a disposable remote.
- AC-5 When a completed predecessor's branch advances past its reviewed and completing commits only by a clean re-merge of its own `integration_target` (the tree equals the automatic merge of its parents, no hand-resolved hunk, the predecessor's record unchanged), `work claim --on <predecessor>@<merge sha>` shall accept the pin as the predecessor's own push accepts that history; a merge of any other branch, a hand-resolved hunk or a record change in the merge shall still refuse naming the cause; evidence: a `work_cli` fixture reproducing #332, failing before the change, and one negative fixture per case. (serves EPC-022 AC-38)
