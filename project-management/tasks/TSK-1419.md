# TSK-1419: The hosted policy check fetches the shared registry before codeflow ci judges a pull request

## Description

TSK-1135's capability impact rule joins legacy catalog entries through the shared id registry's bindings, so the hosted `commit standards` job must fetch `codeflow/registry` before it runs `codeflow ci`. The EPC-026 line made that reorder (commit `aab917de6`, "fetch registry before hosted judging"). The EPC-026 main sync (TSK-1371) takes main's three `codeflow-policy.yml` copies whole, by the Director's ruling of 2026-11-10, because main rewrote the verdict job under the default-branch authority model. That drops the reorder from the line: main's form runs "Fetch the shared registry" after `codeflow ci`.

The reorder is needed only where the capability rule runs. Main's `commit standards` runs on `pull_request_target` and builds codeflow from the default branch's own source (main's `.github/workflows/codeflow-policy.yml:89` and `:181`, "Build codeflow from the authority's source"), so the rule first runs on main when EPC-026 merges there. This task therefore lands on the EPC-026 line after TSK-1371's sync: it moves the "Fetch the shared registry" step (with the "Assert the authority checkout" step after it) to just before "codeflow ci (commit + branch + PR-body standards)" in the line's three copies, and the move reaches main in the same checkpoint merge as the rule. The edit is an operator script, because workflow files are operator-owned. Source: `coordination/coordinators/capabilities/builders/epc026-main-sync-analysis.md` section 3.1; placement by the Director's ruling of 2026-11-10, which replaces the earlier "goes to main separately".

Moving the fetch is not enough on its own. When the registry is absent, the fetch step logs it and goes on (main's `assets/base/ci/codeflow-policy.yml`, the "Fetch the shared registry" step: "codeflow/registry is not on origin; the check reports it when records are tracked"), and the rule reports a failed check at its configured level (`crates/codeflow-cli/src/cmd/ci/capability_impact.rs:74-81`), so at `warn` a run without the registry's bindings still passes. By the Director's ruling of 2026-11-10, made under the workspace rule that a security check fails closed when it cannot be sure, missing or unreadable registry provenance refuses at `warn` and at `block` (AC-4); at `off` the rule does not run. The operator can overrule it as adopter-facing behaviour. A `warn` run that passed without the registry now fails, so this is a breaking change for adopters.

Non-goals: the line's dropped `EXPECTED_*` pre-check and post-run snapshot, which main's own head, base-ref and body-digest checks cover (same section).

## Outcome

After the EPC-026 line merges into main, the hosted `commit standards` job fetches the shared registry before `codeflow ci` in the live workflow, the managed asset and the baseline, so the capability impact rule never judges without the registry's bindings, and a run whose registry is missing or unreadable is refused with a message that says why and how to clear it.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1 In all three `codeflow-policy.yml` copies on the EPC-026 line, the "Fetch the shared registry" step and its "Assert the authority checkout" step shall run before the `codeflow ci` step, the registry shall be fetched once per job, and the three copies shall stay byte-identical where they are today; evidence: the diff and the existing workflow consistency tests passing.
- AC-2 The move shall be made on the line after TSK-1371's main sync lands, on main's form of the job. The only other workflow change shall be the one AC-4 needs in the "Fetch the shared registry" step: it shall tell an absent `codeflow/registry` branch apart from a failed remote lookup or fetch, shall not stop the job before `codeflow ci` judges, and shall hand the outcome to `codeflow ci` so the verdict can name it; evidence: the diff against the line tip that carries TSK-1371, showing only the moved steps and that fetch step change.
- AC-3 A workflow test shall read each of the three copies and fail when "Fetch the shared registry" does not come before the `codeflow ci` step or when the registry is fetched more than once in the job; evidence: the test failing on the line tip before the move and passing after it, with both runs' output in the pull request body.
- AC-4 When the shared registry cannot be fetched or its provenance cannot be read, the capability impact rule shall refuse at `warn` and at `block`, and shall never judge against missing bindings; the refusal message shall name the cause and how to clear it, and shall tell a failed lookup or fetch apart from absent provenance (no `codeflow/registry` branch, or a binding it cannot read); evidence: fixtures that drive the path from the fetch step's outcome to the `codeflow ci` verdict, for a failed fetch and for absent provenance, each at `warn` and at `block`, asserting the exit status and the diagnostic text. The `warn` fixtures fail before the change and pass after; the `block` fixtures, which already refuse before it, show the new diagnostic. (Director ruling of 2026-11-10, under the workspace fail-closed rule.)
