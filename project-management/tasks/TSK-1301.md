# TSK-1301: The pre-push destination probe's timeout and attempt count come from policy

## Description

The pre-push hook and `codeflow ci` read the destination's default branch with `git ls-remote --symref`, and waited a fixed 10 s per attempt (`LS_REMOTE_DEADLINE`, `crates/codeflow-core/src/git/remote_query.rs:15` at the line tip 1cbf73aea). GitHub answered in 11.3 s and 11.6 s on 2026-11-03, so ordinary pushes refused with `ci.destination_unavailable`. This task moves the waits into policy and declares every limit it touches in one place.

- Keys: `git.remote_query_timeout_seconds` (default 30), `git.remote_query_attempts` (default 3) and `git.remote_fetch_timeout_seconds` (default 120). Basis: GitHub's measured 11.3 s and 11.6 s against the old 10 s; 30 s leaves room above that, and 120 s is the earlier fixed fetch wait. Each is a whole number of at least 1. The only upper bound is what a u64 holds; any other value refuses at load, naming the key.
- Declaration: `crates/codeflow-core/src/hooks/limits.rs` declares the three keys, the guard hook timeout (30 s), the guard deadline (20 s, a 10 s buffer under the hook timeout) and the guard network budget (10 s, a 10 s buffer under the deadline), each with a default, bounds, a class (operational or security) and a reason. The policy defaults, the schema check, the loader and the guard constants read only from it, in a shape the limits registry, TSK-1299 ("One limits registry, declared once", EPC-028), absorbs unchanged: the three probe keys and the guard hook timeout, deadline and network budget. TSK-1301 lands first as its first consumer (operator rule, relayed by coord/ci).
- Callers: the pre-push push set, `codeflow ci` (release scope, own-line tip, target tip), `task status` (checkout scope), the id sync's absence check and every fetch of a missing tip use the configured values.
- Guard path, option (b): the session guards ask the remote only while its tracking refs are missing (`landed_policy::advertises_refs`). In that state no policy authority exists, so no configured value can reach a guard. The guard uses its own declared 10 s budget, one attempt, and refuses with a message that names the budget, the 20 s deadline and the 30 s hook timeout and says to run `git fetch <remote>`; with the fetched refs the guard reads the policy locally and asks nothing. Option (a), deriving the guard and harness timeouts from policy at `codeflow update`, was rejected: it rewrites the hook timeouts of three harnesses (Claude, Codex, Grok) and adopters' installed files, which is fragile, and a long guard wait stalls every guarded command. (b) keeps the guard fast and fail-closed.
- Misfit configuration: no configured value reaches the guard (above), and on the pre-push and `ci` paths no harness timeout applies, so no combination of the keys misfits a budget. The validator therefore refuses only values outside each key's declared bounds; the guard clamps to its own budget and fails closed. A validator refusal of a product such as 30 s times 3 attempts would refuse the shipped defaults themselves.
- Threat closed: the waits come from the default branch's committed policy (the local authority, `refs/remotes/<remote>/HEAD`, or the policy at the authority `codeflow ci` judges with), never from the working copy or the pushed head (commit 78a29ee70, "read policy only from the default branch"). A pushed head cannot lengthen or shorten its own lookup, and an unreadable authority falls back to the shipped defaults, never to the working copy. An unreadable destination still refuses.
- Siblings not folded: `crates/codeflow-core/src/coord/observe.rs:54-58` (Git 10 s, network 20 s; TSK-422, ADR-0086) and `crates/codeflow-core/src/ceremony/host.rs:16` (60 s `gh` call; TSK-149) are fail-soft observers: a timeout reports unknown and refuses nothing, so remote latency cannot block work there. Both tasks are complete; TSK-1299 owns moving them into the declaration. `MAX_ANSWER_BYTES` (16 MiB, `remote_query.rs`) is a memory bound, not a wait, and stays a constant for TSK-1299.

## Outcome

After this merges, an adopter whose remote answers slowly can push without refusals, and can tune the wait on the default branch's policy; an agent whose clone lacks tracking refs gets a guard refusal that names the fetch that clears it.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1 When the destination does not answer within the configured wait and attempts, the pre-push hook and `codeflow ci` shall still refuse with `ci.destination_unavailable`; evidence: `pre_push_waits_as_long_as_the_default_branch_policy_says` (its 1 s case) in `crates/codeflow-cli/tests/policy_authority_journey.rs`.
- AC-2 The per-attempt wait, the attempt count and the fetch wait shall come from `git.remote_query_timeout_seconds`, `git.remote_query_attempts` and `git.remote_fetch_timeout_seconds`, defaulting to 30, 3 and 120, declared once with default, bounds, class and reason; evidence: `crates/codeflow-core/src/hooks/limits.rs` and its test, `remote_query_keys_accept_whole_numbers_from_one_and_refuse_the_rest`.
- AC-3 When a key holds zero, a negative or fractional number, a value over what a u64 holds or a non-number, the validator and the loader shall refuse it, naming the key, and nothing shall wrap or panic; evidence: `remote_query_keys_accept_whole_numbers_from_one_and_refuse_the_rest`, and the largest values in `a_slow_destination_answers_within_the_policy_timeout`.
- AC-4 A timed-out lookup's refusal shall name the wait, the attempts, the measured time and the keys; evidence: `a_timeout_names_the_limits_the_measured_time_and_the_keys` and the journey test's 1 s case.
- AC-5 An answer slower than the old fixed wait shall refuse under that wait and pass under the shipped default; evidence: `a_slow_destination_answers_within_the_policy_timeout` (11.3 s against 10 s and 30 s, scaled by 50), the journey test (1.5 s against 1 s and 3 s) and a real-time run of a fake 11.3 s destination: the 3.1.0 binary refused after 10.4 s, this build passed after 11.8 s.
- AC-6 Every caller of the remote query shall use the configured values: the pre-push push set, `codeflow ci`, `task status`, the id sync and the fetch of a missing tip; evidence: the PR diff.
- AC-7 The keys shall be read from the default branch's committed policy, never from the working copy or a pushed head; evidence: `push_set::probe_limits`, `ci::run` (the `limits` of `Names`) and the journey test, whose task branch's working policy sets no keys while the default branch's does.
- AC-8 (journey) When the remote's tracking refs are missing and the remote never answers, a guarded command shall refuse inside the guard's 10 s budget, before its 20 s deadline, naming the budget and `git fetch <remote>`; after that fetch the same command shall pass with the remote still silent; evidence: `the_guard_refuses_a_silent_remote_inside_its_budget_and_a_fetch_clears_it`.
- AC-9 The policy reference, the destination remedy and the changelog shall name the keys, their defaults and bounds, and the guard's budget with the fetch that clears it; evidence: `docs/policy-reference.md`, `crates/codeflow-core/src/remedy.rs`, `changelog.d/TSK-1301.md`.
