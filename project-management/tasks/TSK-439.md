# TSK-439: Streams, lanes and seats carry delegated work across coordinators and harnesses

## Description

ADR-0086, the lanes and roles part, as the operator decided on
2026-10-22 and the Codex review settled (F02, F03, F10 to F12, F21),
with the operator's later requirement (the design page's work streams
section) that
several coordinating sessions can run at once, within one harness and
across harnesses, each managing its own lanes for a different body of
work. A session is a process; a stream is a body of work with one live
coordinator; the coordinator is the lead role of a stream; a lane is a
durable, harness-neutral unit of delegated work inside a stream; a seat is the harness
session attached to it, replaceable; an attempt is one run of work
inside a lane with its own branch and worktree. Today a lane exists only
as a Herdr tab and a conversation, so a deleted session loses which
seats were running and which reviews were in flight.

Outcome: **a work stream `WS-NNN-<slug>` is local to the ledger with a
descriptive scope (an epic, several epics or a topic) and one live
coordinator held by lease and generation; a lane has a local display id `LN-NNN-<slug>`, belongs to exactly one stream, and has a descriptive
scope (task, tasks, epic or topic, with refs) that changes as events,
attempts that each record task, worktree, branch, exact revision,
expected result location and native provenance, seats with a recorded
kind (external, resumable or in-process), a `lane_reported` result, and
a lifecycle open, active, idle and closed (done with evidence, abandoned
or superseded naming its successor); the coordinator role is a lease
with an actor instance id and a generation; takeover is only by explicit
`adopt` after an inventory; a stale holder is reported, never taken; and
a new coordinator on any harness keeps the same lane numbers for the
same work and continues from disk.**

Rules:

- **Streams.** `WS-NNN-<slug>` is reserved ahead of use like FU and LN
  and local to the ledger; `stream_opened` records a descriptive scope
  (links to an epic, several epics or a topic phrase; never an
  amendment of any record), `stream_scope_changed` a change with its
  reason, `stream_closed` a reason and a disposition for its open lanes
  and follow-ups. One live coordinator per stream, held by lease and
  generation; a session names the stream it coordinates when it
  claims, and the start digest never guesses one. Every lane belongs to
  exactly one stream and only that stream's coordinator opens or closes
  it; a lane moves between streams only by a recorded handover both
  coordinators make (`lane_handed_over`, two events, one per
  coordinator). A task is active in one stream's lane at a time; a
  second stream starting it gets a warning naming the owner, and claims
  stay advisory. A lone session gets a default stream automatically.
  Adoption and harness switching are per stream. Every coordinator
  event carries its stream.
- **Identity.** `LN-NNN` is reserved ahead of use in `counters.json`,
  rising, never reused, local to the ledger instance; the slug is
  presentation, derived from the scope, lowercase and short, unique
  among open lanes, renamable; labels and seat names carry a bounded
  stable `LN-NNN` (within Herdr's 32-character name limit), never the
  scope prose.
- **Scope is descriptive only.** `lane_opened` records kind (build,
  review, research, consult), scope (kind `task`, `tasks`, `epic` or
  `topic` with refs) and purpose; `lane_scope_changed` records a new
  scope and reason as an event, so the history shows which work the
  lane carried. A scope change never amends criteria, dependencies or
  approved assignments, which keep their tracked process. Only the
  coordinator opens lanes (decided). Readiness applies to starting
  implementation, through the same core as `work start`, including
  reviewed stacks and standalone tasks: a `task` or `tasks` lane starts
  a task when `work next` reports it ready, an `epic` lane takes the
  epic's tasks in order as each becomes ready, a `topic` lane builds
  nothing until it creates or links tasks. Review and consult lanes
  bind an exact revision and never re-claim the task.
- **Attempts.** `seat_attached` records an attempt: task, worktree,
  branch, exact revision, expected result location, requested and
  observed model and effort, and native provenance where the transport
  offers it (`codeflow delegate` run and turn records, native thread or
  resume ids; unknowns explicit). Every implementation attempt keeps
  its own task branch and worktree; a lane may hold several attempts.
  `lane_reported` records a result as reported, harvested or accepted,
  with its reference. In-session workers are recorded under their
  parent attempt, not as lanes; a crashed parent cannot resume them by
  replaying ids.
- **Seat kinds.** `external` (a CLI in a Herdr pane) survives its
  coordinator and is re-checked live (pane, cwd, native session,
  ownership) before anything is sent or closed; `resumable` (a native
  resume id) is resumed in its own harness when available; `in-process`
  (an Agent-tool subagent, an in-app worker) dies with its parent, so
  the attempt is marked lost and the recovery inventory runs.
- **Seats the new coordinator cannot reach** (the operator's case:
  lanes ran as Claude desktop sessions and the coordinator restarts in
  Codex, which can neither resume nor drive them). Such a seat is never
  assumed dead: it is judged by its own recorded heartbeat and reports,
  because the shared file is the channel and a live seat keeps writing
  `lane_reported` and refreshing its status through `codeflow`
  commands. Fresh: leave it running, keep reading its reports, start no
  duplicate writer on its worktree. Silent past the heartbeat timeout:
  the timeout triggers investigation, never replacement by itself,
  because a silent seat is not proof its work stopped (a long command
  outlives the timeout and may still write its worktree). The recovery
  inventory runs (branch, worktree, revision, expected result, dirty
  state, in-flight operations). Replacement on the same branch and
  worktree needs stopped-work evidence (the seat's harness session
  ended, its process gone, its worktree removed, or its last report
  says it finished) or an explicit resolution of the overlap under
  AC-5 (a new branch and worktree for the new attempt, or the operator
  stopping the old seat); only then is `seat_detached` recorded with
  reason `lost` and a new seat attached in the current harness that
  continues from the task record, the branch and the worktree, in its
  own attempt. With neither, the seat is kept as unresolved and
  possibly live, no duplicate writer starts on its resources, and the
  coordinator says so in its status. An old seat that comes back finds
  the lane adopted and stops. A resume id is a recovery lead, never
  permission to drive a matching pane.
- **Coordinator lease.** `coordinator_claimed` binds an actor instance
  id and a generation; `coordinator_released` records a hand-over;
  `coordinator_adopted` and `lane_adopted` compare the expected holder,
  generation and the observed last heartbeat under the lock, then
  increment the generation; a stale writer is refused at the operation
  that can check it. A lease is coordination ownership, not an
  execution lock: before replacement work starts, the old seat's live
  panes, in-flight operations, branches and dirty worktrees are
  inventoried, anything not shown stopped is kept and overlapping work
  needs explicit resolution; nothing is cleaned up from a timeout. A
  returning session that finds its role or lane adopted stops and says
  so. Two coordinators by accident: the first claim wins; the second is
  told who leads and works as a lane or observer.
- **Timing.** `check interval` (default 10 minutes) and `heartbeat
  timeout` (default 30 minutes) are advisory project settings placed
  by TSK-280's rule, the timeout longer than the interval, per stream
  for the coordinator lease; the caller's status file is refreshed by
  every bound mutating operation (`follow add|update|check|hand-over|
  close|drop|reopen`, `lane open|attach|scope|report|adopt|close`,
  `coordinator claim|release|adopt`, `work claim`, `work start`, and
  the turn-end check of a bound session), never by a read-only surface
  (`follow list`, `coord status`, `lane list`, `lane inventory`,
  `session-orient`, any status read, which write nothing); an unbound
  observer (a session that has claimed no role) reads only and gets no
  heartbeat; liveness uses monotonic elapsed time within a valid clock
  context and treats a clock discontinuity as uncertain; expiry proves
  contact overdue ("operation may still be running"), never death.
- **Switching.** A new coordinator on any harness or model keeps the
  same lane for the same work and attaches a new seat that continues
  from the attempt's branch and worktree, never from the old
  conversation; a lane closes as superseded, naming its successor and
  handing over its open follow-ups, only when the scope changes, the
  state cannot be recovered or the operator restarts it. Review
  independence is judged against the work's author, not the
  coordinator; a changed model on an attempt rechecks the lineage rule.
  Lane `done` needs result evidence and a disposition for each open
  follow-up; abandonment hands them over, never completes them.
- **Hooks into existing commands.** `work claim` keeps its verdicts,
  prints a hint when no lane holds the task and links a lane given with
  `--lane`; `cf-herdr` seat open takes `--lane LN-NNN` or a recorded
  `--no-lane "<reason>"` (consults, canaries, evaluations and the
  operator's own seats), and its live identity checks still decide.
- **Recovery inventory** (the issue comment's third point): a read-only
  `codeflow lane inventory` (provisional) lists, per lane and attempt,
  the worktree state, unpushed and unreferenced commits, the last seat
  and its kind, and what is not recoverable.

Non-goals: the hooks (TSK-440); the rules (TSK-423); nested duos
(the peer and worker prohibition stays); cross-machine lanes (an
external reference only).

Amended 2026-10-31 by the hierarchy amendment (ADR-0094, accepted by the
operator that day): this task also carries the ledger side of the
Director, Coordinator and Lane hierarchy, with no role check in the
tooling. A stream gains an optional `parent`, so a Director's stream has
Coordinator streams under it, and `stream open --parent WS-NNN` reserves
the id through the store. A coordinator claim and adoption carry the
holder's `harness`, `session_name` and `resume_id`, and a seat carries
`session_name` and `resume_id`, so every level is a named, resumable
session: `coord rename|resume` and `lane rename|resume` record and look
them up, and `resume` never launches. The schema moves to version 2 in
one cutover with the report task (TSK-1126), which extends the same
files after this task lands; binaries built between the two, the hook
binary on `PATH` included, stay off the shared live ledger. The
`stream_reported` event, the report body and the report identity rule are
TSK-1126's; `lane report` with `summary` stays here. Depth stays at two
supervision levels as guidance: a stream with a parent takes no children,
and the tree view (TSK-440) shows depth rather than refusing.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1 `codeflow lane open`, by the coordinator only, shall reserve `LN-NNN` ahead of use (never reused after a close), derive a bounded slug from the scope and append `lane_opened` with kind, scope (task, tasks, epic or topic with refs) and purpose; `lane scope` shall append `lane_scope_changed` and rename the slug; a scope change shall never alter a task's criteria, dependencies or assignment; and `list --history` shall show which work the lane carried; evidence: CLI tests, including a scope change and a refused open from a lane. (serves EPC-025 AC-2)
- AC-2 `lane attach` shall append `seat_attached` with the seat kind (external, resumable, in-process), the seat's optional `session_name` and `resume_id`, and an attempt (task, worktree, branch, exact revision, expected result location, requested and observed model and effort, native provenance or explicit unknowns), each implementation attempt shall keep its own branch and worktree, `lane report` shall append `lane_reported` with a `summary`, marked reported, harvested or accepted with a reference that names the `sequence` of the reported event it marks (the optional body, the retry-key rule and `stream_reported` are TSK-1126's), a lost or replaced seat shall append `seat_detached` with its reason, and in-session workers shall be recorded under their parent attempt; evidence: CLI tests and a delegate-run fixture. (serves EPC-025 AC-2, AC-6)
- AC-3 A `task` or `tasks` lane shall start a task only when `work next` reports it ready, through the same core as `work start` (reviewed stacks and standalone tasks included), an `epic` lane shall take tasks in the epic's order as each becomes ready, a `topic` lane shall refuse to build until it creates or links a task, and a review or consult lane shall bind an exact revision without re-claiming the task; evidence: one CLI test per kind. (serves EPC-025 AC-2)
- AC-4 `coordinator claim --stream WS-NNN` shall bind an actor instance id and generation to that stream and record the holder's optional `harness`, `session_name` and `resume_id` on `coordinator_claimed` (a lone session getting a default stream, which appears by the same open-then-claim order AC-10 states), two coordinators of different streams shall run at once in one harness and across harnesses without touching each other's lanes, a second claim on the same stream shall be told who leads,  `release` shall record a hand-over, `adopt` (coordinator or lane) shall compare the expected holder, generation and observed last heartbeat under the lock and increment the generation, a stale writer shall be refused at the operation that can check it, a second coordinator shall be told who leads and refused coordinator writes, and a returning session that finds its role or lane adopted shall stop and say so; evidence: concurrency tests with an interleaved renew and adopt, and a return after adoption. (serves EPC-025 AC-2)
- AC-5 Before replacement work starts, `adopt` shall run the inventory of the old seat's live panes, in-flight operations, branches and dirty worktrees, keep anything not shown stopped, require explicit resolution of overlapping work and never clean up from a timeout; `lane inventory` shall list per lane and attempt the worktree state, unpushed and unreferenced commits, the last seat and its kind, and what is not recoverable; evidence: a lost-transcript fixture with two in-flight reviews and a removed worktree. (serves EPC-025 AC-2)
- AC-6 `check interval` (default 10 minutes) and `heartbeat timeout` (default 30 minutes) shall be advisory project settings, a timeout not longer than the interval shall be refused naming both, every bound mutating operation and the turn-end check of a bound session shall refresh the caller's status file while read-only surfaces write nothing and an unbound observer gets no heartbeat, staleness shall use monotonic time within a valid clock context and report a clock discontinuity as uncertain, and a coordinator inside a 45-minute gate shall show as contact overdue with the operation possibly running, never as abandoned or adopted; evidence: settings tests, a refresh test per bound operation, a zero-write test per read-only surface and a clock-change test. (serves EPC-025 AC-2)
- AC-7 Each scenario of the design's switching table shall hold: same harness new session (adopt with the next generation, everything as it was); Claude to Codex coordinator with Herdr seats (external seats untouched after live checks); Claude to Codex with in-process subagents (attempts lost, inventory, new seats continue from disk); a seat the new harness cannot reach (lanes that ran as Claude desktop sessions, the coordinator restarted in Codex): fresh by its own heartbeat and reports, it is left running, its reports read and no duplicate writer started on its worktree; silent past the timeout, the inventory runs and replacement on the same branch and worktree happens only with stopped-work evidence or an explicit resolution of the overlap under AC-5, with `seat_detached` recorded and a new seat in the current harness continuing from the task record, branch and worktree in its own attempt, while a seat with neither is kept as unresolved and possibly live and reported as such, and the old seat on return finds the lane adopted and stops; - AC-8 A lane shall close as `done` only with result evidence and a disposition for each open follow-up, as `abandoned` with its follow-ups handed over, or as `superseded` naming its successor only when the scope changes, the state cannot be recovered or the operator restarts it; the same lane number shall be kept for the same work on a new seat; `work claim` shall keep its verdicts, print the no-lane hint and link `--lane`; and `cf-herdr` seat open shall require `--lane` or a recorded `--no-lane` reason, carry the lane id in the label and keep its live identity checks; evidence: CLI tests and a native Herdr canary. - AC-10 `stream open`, `scope` and `close` shall append their events with a descriptive scope that never amends a record, every lane shall belong to exactly one stream and be opened or closed only by that stream's coordinator, a lane shall move between streams only by a recorded handover both coordinators make, a task already active in one stream's lane shall produce a warning naming the owner when a second stream starts it while the claim stays advisory, and adoption shall be per stream;
