# TSK-101: shared id registry with uid binding and the merge rule

## Description

Build the registry of Plan v4 section 2 so that ids stay unique across
checkouts, worktrees and lines. Every record gains a hidden UUIDv4 `uid`;
numbers are issued by a compare-and-swap push to `codeflow/registry` and
chosen from the registry's whole history and every visible ref; every record
added in a pull request must be bound to its number before merge; and the
registry only grows, enforced by pre-push, git-guard and a registry CI job.
It includes `codeflow ids seed`, `backfill`, `sync`, `admit`, `retarget` and
`check`, `task new --resume`, the `doctor` report, and the `codeflow remote
protect` data profile as product code (SPC-013 planning resolution 12).
Seeding gives every
existing record a `uid`, legacy `TSK-NNN-NNN` included, with no legacy
exemption. CodeFlow's registry continues at EPC-020, SPC-013, TSK-100 and
the next ADR number. Non-goal: exclusive claims (EPC-021) and the per-line
backfill PRs (TSK-109).

## Affected surfaces and interfaces

- `crates/codeflow-core/src/workgraph/` (allocation, store, record schema)
  and the `epic new`, `spec new` and `task new` commands (CAP-010).
- New `codeflow ids` commands; `doctor` (CAP-008).
- Pre-push and git-guard rules for `codeflow/registry`; the scaffolded CI
  workflow (merge rule and registry job); `codeflow remote protect` data
  profile (CAP-003, CAP-008).
- Shared files this task edits: the CI workflow, the hooks, the policy
  schema and the record templates (`uid`). Ownership follows SPC-013
  planning resolution 8.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] AC-1 When `epic new`, `spec new` or `task new` creates a record, the CLI
      shall fetch `codeflow/registry` without force, take one more than the
      highest id ever added in the registry history and on every local and
      remote-tracking ref, push the reservation without force, and write the
      record with exclusive create and the reservation's `uid`; evidence:
      concurrent issue journeys from two worktrees and two clones.
- [ ] AC-2 When the push is rejected because the tip moved, the CLI shall
      fetch, recompute and retry a bounded number of times, then stop with
      the reason; permission, unsupported and transport errors shall be
      reported as such and never as "taken"; evidence: one fault test per
      outcome.
- [ ] AC-3 When the push result is unclear, the CLI shall treat the number as
      reserved only if the fetched tip binds it to its own `uid`; evidence:
      the lost acknowledgement journey.
- [ ] AC-4 When offline, the CLI shall commit the reservation on the local
      `codeflow/registry` under a lock and report "pending, not unique yet";
      `ids sync` shall publish it, treat the same `uid` as already reserved,
      and stop on a different `uid` with a direction to run `ids retarget`,
      which renumbers the unmerged record and its in-tree links and records
      `former_ids`; evidence: the offline, sync and retarget journey.
- [ ] AC-5 `ids admit` shall reserve a number for a fork or hand-written
      record's `uid`, or the next free number with a retarget direction;
      `task new --resume TSK-N` shall write the record only when the
      reservation's issuer and `uid` match; no number is ever reissued;
      evidence: the admission and crash-between-reserve-and-write journeys.
- [ ] AC-6 When a pull request adds a record not bound in the authority's
      registry to its `uid`, or edits the `uid` of an existing record, CI
      shall block; CI shall fetch `codeflow/registry` explicitly with enough
      history in shallow and single-branch checkouts; evidence: CI fixtures,
      including the registry moving between the check and the merge.
- [ ] AC-7 When a pushed range on `codeflow/registry` removes or modifies a
      file, or adds one outside `ids/`, pre-push and git-guard shall refuse
      it, the registry CI job shall report any such commit in history, and
      allocation shall refuse to issue while damage is unrepaired; evidence:
      refused-push tests and a job fixture.
- [ ] AC-8 On a host without branch rules, the CLI shall refuse to issue when
      the fetched tip does not descend from the last verified tip, and
      `doctor` shall report reduced assurance; evidence: the rewrite
      detection journey.
- [ ] AC-9 `ids seed` shall write one file per id found on every ref and in
      retained history, give each record one `uid` (replicas decided by
      first introducing commit, or first landing on a stable target where
      squash or cherry-pick hides it), and report every id it cannot place;
      `ids backfill` shall copy the registered `uid` into records on the
      current line and never generate one; evidence: tests on a fixture with
      replicas, a squash, a cherry-pick and the legacy form.
- [ ] AC-10 `codeflow/registry` shall stay out of `protected_branches`,
      branch naming and the product test gate, shall never trigger `ids sync`
      recursively, and `remote protect` shall apply its data profile without
      changing `main`'s rules; evidence: policy and remote-protect tests.
- [ ] AC-11 (journey) On a fresh `codeflow init` project with a remote and on
      a no-remote project, using the installed CLI, issue, sync, admission
      and the two-integration-lines case shall produce unique numbers, and a
      deleted top reservation shall not be reissued; evidence: the Plan v4
      section 10 registry journeys, each with a passing control and a fault.

  the issued number exceeds every number ever added and is never reissued;
  broad state space of adds, deletions and concurrent pushes), and a
  targeted mutation run on the append-only range check and the merge-rule
  binding (guard and validator logic whose wrong decision is invisible).
- Recovery: the registry is append-only; a bad reservation is left as a gap
  and a record is retargeted, never renumbered after landing.

  review-relevant. A material graph, scope, interface, ownership, acceptance
  or safety change stops work and returns to both primary seats as Plan v5
  before implementation continues. Closeout cannot approve a deviation after
  the fact.
