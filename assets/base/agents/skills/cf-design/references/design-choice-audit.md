# Design-choice audit

Use this reference after forming a candidate direction to find material choices
that lack product evidence. It is not a checklist, theme catalog, scoring
system, or set of prohibited styles. Familiar patterns may be exactly right;
the failure is accepting or rejecting one without establishing how it serves
this product, audience, content, context, and platform.

The examples describe the shape of an unexamined decision, not an inventory of
formats to avoid. Absence from this reference is not evidence that a choice has
been examined.

## How to use this reference

1. Identify a material choice in the proposed direction.
2. Ask what product, audience, content, or platform evidence supports it.
3. Keep it when it is the clearest fit and record the rationale.
4. Refine or replace it when it is merely the easiest available completion.
5. Do not reject an operator-requested or familiar direction by category.
   Give evidence-backed counsel, then respect the settled direction.

## Decision patterns to examine

### Borrowed structure without product logic

- a conventional page sequence substituted for the user's actual decision
  journey;
- sections or groups given equal weight despite unequal information priority;
- numbered or staged sections whose order carries no information;
- proof, endorsements, metrics, or claims that were never supplied.

Inspect whether structure follows the subject, content, and next decisions.
Never invent proof to complete a familiar composition.

### Category shorthand without evidence

- palette, typography, density, imagery, or tone selected mainly to signal a
  product category rather than express this brief;
- a visual register carried over from reference material without checking
  brand, content, audience, or use-context fit;
- language or imagery whose implied qualities conflict with the product's
  actual value or operating conditions.

Any familiar register can be appropriate. State the evidence that makes it
specific to this product.

### Grouping without information semantics

- every fact isolated into an equal container;
- visually varied group sizes without an information-priority rationale;
- charts, badges, or status treatments added because the composition expects
  them;
- a desktop grouping compressed rather than reconsidered for a smaller
  viewport or different input mode.

Use components and grouping when they express meaningful boundaries, states, or
actions. Structure is information, not a decoration layer.

### Metaphor or decoration without behavior

- simulated device, application, document, or control chrome that implies
  behavior the product does not have;
- decorative data, controls, grids, or status signals that users could mistake
  for real state;
- effects that weaken hierarchy, contrast, legibility, or platform fit without
  advancing the intended experience.

Ask what the metaphor communicates, whether that communication is true, and
whether a simpler expression would lose anything material.

### Motion without an interaction purpose

- identical reveal animation on every element;
- parallax, cursor effects, or ambient movement without a product purpose;
- motion that hides loading or state changes instead of explaining them.

Prefer one purposeful moment over scattered effects. Respect reduced-motion
preferences and preserve comprehension without animation.

### System inertia or novelty without need

- an existing treatment repeated where the content or interaction has a
  materially different need;
- a new visual language or abstraction introduced where the accepted system
  already serves the brief;
- platform conventions ignored either for cross-platform sameness or novelty.

Existing systems deserve a strong presumption, not immunity from evidence.
Extend them coherently when the product has a real unmet need.

## Critique lenses

Use the lenses that fit the surface:

- **Brief fit:** does the direction express the creator's actual intent?
- **Audience fit:** does it support the user's job, context, trust, and
  expectations?
- **Hierarchy:** does structure show what matters and what happens next?
- **Interaction:** are state, feedback, recovery, and destructive actions clear?
- **Specificity:** can material choices be traced to this subject rather than
  category shorthand?
- **Coherence:** do type, colour, layout, imagery, density, and motion reinforce
  one direction?
- **Restraint:** does every visual and interactive device earn its place?
- **System fit:** does it extend existing foundations rather than fragment them?
- **Accessibility:** does the design work for relevant input, perception,
  cognition, viewport, and motion needs?
- **Fidelity:** does the implementation preserve the settled direction and
  behavior?

State the evidence behind a criticism. “This is generic,” “this is familiar,”
or “I dislike this” alone is not a review finding.
